# Published BIA/AG/AAO decision index: feasibility findings

Research and implementation snapshot: 2026-08-31. This subsystem is intentionally separate from the INASearch application.

## Bottom line

The index is feasible, and EOIR's bound reporters provide a much better treatment seed than a case-text parser alone: a formal “affected decisions” table with coded entries for clarified, distinguished, explained, followed, generally cited, followed only in a jurisdiction, modified, not followed, overruled, reaffirmed, superseded, and vacated decisions. It is historical rather than current, so no single free **authoritative and bulk-reusable** source still provides all three things INASearch needs: authoritative published decision text, normalized law-to-case applicability, and comprehensive present-day later-treatment status. A specialist commercial/licensable candidate now exists: BIA Edge's ImmCite offers current immigration-specific status signals and a product-data license. It should be evaluated as a vendor evidence layer, not substituted for the first-party corpus.

The authoritative electronic text sources are strong from volume 8 forward and for USCIS-adopted AAO decisions. Later-treatment status should start with the reporter table and then be extended as an evidence-bearing graph from subsequent opinions, statutes, regulations, and publisher status statements. EOIR's topical indexes and 1995-2017 BIA Precedent Chart improve recall but do not answer current validity. Commercial citators may be useful as a later licensed cross-check, but their editorial status is not a distributable source corpus and their labels should not replace decision text.

## Source coverage

- [EOIR's AG/BIA decisions index](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/ag-bia-decisions), its five official alphabetical lists, and the linked DOJ PDFs provide published precedent in electronic reporter volumes 8 through the current volume. The discovery parser currently identifies 3,296 records and requires every explicit official PDF entry to parse or fail visibly.
- Three of those listings currently terminate in first-party 404s despite duplicate links in EOIR's live and archived catalogs: *Pizarro*, 12 I&N Dec. 537; *Reyes*, 13 I&N Dec. 406; and *Lee*, 16 I&N Dec. 305. The corpus has authoritative metadata for all 3,296 EOIR records and official PDF bytes for 3,293. The three exceptions remain explicit metadata-only gaps pending publisher repair or acceptance of a separately labeled preservation surrogate.
- [USCIS's adopted AAO decisions page](https://www.uscis.gov/about-us/organization/directorates-and-program-offices/administrative-appeals-office-aao/adopted-aao-decisions) provides 24 current and prior adopted-decision packages. Its own current/prior and overruled/superseded statements are captured separately from treatment inferred from cases.
- Non-precedent AAO decisions and unpublished BIA decisions are excluded.
- [EOIR's official volume 25 affected-decisions notice](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/eoir/legacy/2014/05/29/Notice%20Regarding%20Admin%20and%20Judicial%20Decisions%20%5Brevised%20per%20DLN%205.23.14%5D.pdf) is a 37-page searchable reporter table revised May 23, 2014. The checksummed snapshot parses to 2,145 coded rows and 2,653 treatment/pinpoint edges. All but one treating pinpoint resolve to an electronic-manifest case; the one unresolved printed reference (`21-3371`) is retained without a guessed repair.
- EOIR's current electronic case index does not expose volumes 1-7. EOIR does publish first-party cumulative topical indexes through volume 27. The volumes 1-15 indexes total 74 image-only pages at about 315 DPI; the volumes 16-25 and 26-27 indexes total 57 searchable pages. All four files are checksummed in `supporting/manifest.json`. The native-text parser currently resolves all 8,784 subject occurrences to 1,425 official-manifest cases, producing 8,720 unique case/topic/detail links. These associations improve topical recall but do not themselves establish a holding. The landing page calls the last index “Volumes 26-28,” while its own title page expressly says volumes 26 and 27 and IDs 3766-3984; the narrower PDF-backed coverage is retained. HathiTrust preserves complete public-domain scans of the GPO volumes, corroborated by the [Library of Congress serial cataloging record](https://www.loc.gov/aba/pcc/conser/CCM/Module34.pdf) and the [Online Books Page serial archive](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/serial?id=admdecimmnat). Those scans are viable as a separately labeled library-surrogate tier after complete capture and case-boundary reconciliation; they are not yet mixed into the authoritative case manifest.

## Is there an existing “still controlling” index?

There is an official historical index, but there is no discovered free, authoritative **current and comprehensive** BIA/AAO citator.

- The formal volume 25 notice covers decisions cited in volumes 16-25. Its codes expressly include `o` for overruled, `m` for modified, `s` for superseded, `v` for vacated, and `n` for not followed. The left and right references can be reporter pinpoints inside decisions, so the parser preserves the written page and resolves it to the containing case instead of assuming every number is a case's first page.
- The notice directs readers to volume 15 for the cumulative volumes 1-15 table. A public-domain GPO volume scan is verified in [HathiTrust's catalog](https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/001719567); the relevant notice occupies scan sequences 23-30 (printed pages XIX-XXVI). It is not yet ingested because the available viewer exposes one page at a time and restricts saving/redistribution; `archival-source-candidates.json` records four stable volume-15 copies and the exact page range.
- USCIS's own adjudicator research guidance explains how officers should use the table, says the later decision must be read to determine whether the affected issue still matters, and requires a separate volume 15 check for older cases. It also states the decisive limitation: the table is not updated when a later statute or regulation renders a case moot. See the “Determining Whether a Case Is Still Valid Law” section of the [USCIS training package](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/foia/National_Interest_Waiver_Adjudication_Guidance.pdf).
- No post-volume-25 affected-decisions table was found in EOIR's current electronic materials. The later cumulative topical index expressly sends readers back to volumes 15 and 25; it is not a treatment update.
- EOIR also provides the opinions, publisher headnotes, cumulative topical indexes through volume 27, and a separate [BIA Precedent Chart](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/bia-precedent-chart) covering volumes 21-27 through July 2017. The chart contains useful later-treatment annotations but expressly says it is informational, noncomprehensive, and not an official EOIR publication. It is therefore a high-value secondary recall source, not a current citator.
- EOIR's alphabetical listings themselves explicitly label a small set of entries as overruled, overruled in part, superseded, vacated, or modified. Seventeen notes identify a later reporter citation and now produce directed publisher-catalog edges: 5 modified, 5 overruled, 1 overruled in part, 1 superseded, and 5 vacated. Two additional PDF-backed historical overrides are marked superseded without a structured target. The written note is retained, but the listings do not apply comparable status review to every reporter case.
- USCIS provides unusually useful status labeling for its small adopted-AAO set, but that does not cover the BIA reporter.
- [CourtListener's I&N reporter browser](https://www.courtlistener.com/c/i-n-dec/) exposes volumes 8-29 and can supply citation links as a secondary recall check. A citation link does not say whether the later case followed, limited, distinguished, or overruled the earlier case, and CourtListener is not used here as authoritative source text.
- **BIA Edge / ImmCite is the closest already-made immigration-specific index found.** Its published methodology rates BIA, Attorney General, Supreme Court, and published circuit decisions as Valid, Caution, Negative, Overruled, Vacated, No public signal, or N/A by examining later decisions, orders, citing authorities, and the authority hierarchy. Its live inventory reports 3,297 published BIA precedents and 18,166 ImmCite validity records across its larger corpus as of August 29, 2026. The vendor offers live or bulk product licensing for ImmCite status data, citation links, holdings, and source-specific refreshes. Public access is useful for evaluation, but the terms prohibit bulk extraction or redistribution without a separate license, the service warns that coverage can be incomplete or delayed, and some classifications are AI-assisted. See [How ImmCite Works](https://biaedge.com/immcite), the [live corpus inventory](https://biaedge.com/corpus), [licensing](https://biaedge.com/licensing), and [terms](https://biaedge.com/terms). A public-surface smoke test found the Delcarmen-Lara update and USCIS Policy Manual alert but returned no case result for either `Arrabally` or `25 I&N Dec. 771`, and the public overruled-BIA view did not expose the case. That may be a public-search/indexing defect rather than a licensed-feed omission, but it makes a contractual coverage test mandatory.
- General commercial systems remain important comparators. Lexis documents a dedicated “Immigration Precedent Decisions: BIA, AAO/AAU” source and exposes Shepard's reports; Westlaw says KeyCite covers selected federal administrative decisions and can flag partial overruling. Their exact immigration coverage should be tested case-by-case during QA. These are licensed editorial research outputs, not authoritative bulk corpora that can simply be republished inside INASearch. See the [Lexis BIA/AAO source instructions](https://supportcenter.lexisnexis.com/app/answers/answer_view/a_id/1088225/~/finding-board-of-immigration-appeals-decisions) and [Westlaw KeyCite description](https://legal.thomsonreuters.com/en/products/westlaw/keycite).
- [BIAcases](https://biacases.com/) is a useful full-text search service covering precedent and unpublished BIA decisions, but its public surface does not publish a treatment methodology or a reusable good-law index. It is a retrieval comparator, not a substitute for ImmCite, Shepard's, KeyCite, or the evidence graph.

Accordingly, INASearch does **not** need to construct the historical graph from zero. It should run a build-versus-license evaluation against BIA Edge/ImmCite, Shepard's, and KeyCite, using the same stratified precedent sample. Even if ImmCite is licensed, INASearch should keep its own evidence graph so every displayed warning can link to first-party text and survive a vendor outage or contract change. A licensed status becomes a separately labeled vendor edge, never source truth and never an unqualified `good law` boolean. Each graph edge stores the later case, treatment verb/code, reporter pinpoints, quoted evidence, affected legal target when identifiable, temporal scope, resolution method, source tier, and confidence.

A vendor proof should be scored against reproducible known answers before purchase: all 17 structured negative/modified relationships printed in EOIR's live alphabetical catalog; a stratified sample of the official volume-25 codes; the prospective-only *Delcarmen-Lara*/*Arrabally* relationship; USCIS's current, superseded, and overruled adopted-AAO labels; and unchanged cases that should not receive a false negative. Acceptance also requires case-level and proposition-level evidence, update latency, historical snapshots, stable identifiers, a machine-readable feed, and contractual permission to show derived warnings in INASearch. A top-level badge without the treating authority and scope does not satisfy the use case.

The advance-parole example demonstrates the combined design. *Matter of Arrabally and Yerrabelly*, 25 I&N Dec. 771 (BIA 2012), falls at the historical table's cutoff, so that table cannot record its later fate. The current opinion parser identifies *Matter of Delcarmen-Lara*, 29 I&N Dec. 830 (BIA 2026), as expressly overruling it and separately preserves the later decision's prospective-only application. EOIR's topical index independently links Arrabally to “departure from United States: pursuant to advance parole,” unlawful-presence inadmissibility, adjustment admissibility, and statutory construction. This is a useful model of historical seed + current incremental treatment + rule/topic context.

## Policy manuals do not eliminate the use case

Agency manuals are the primary operational surface for many officers, and some holdings are incorporated into them. That makes a case annotation more valuable when it explains why a current rule or policy text has a particular meaning, but it also raises the noise threshold.

The initial product should therefore surface only high-confidence links: publisher-headnote authorities, holding-language tied to a specific statute/regulation, and explicit later treatment. Mere citation or background discussion should remain searchable but should not create a default footnote. The current schema distinguishes `holding-candidate` from `discussed` for this reason.

## Implemented evidence model

The local builder now maintains:

1. immutable source PDF bytes and checksums;
2. publisher metadata/headnotes and publisher status as distinct evidence;
3. page-aligned native text plus an optional, non-destructive OCR layer;
4. exact INA/USC/CFR citation spans resolved against INASearch's canonical hierarchy;
5. auditable contextual OCR repairs accepted only when a real legal unit validates;
6. directed treatment edges for explicit language such as *overruled*, *modified*, *clarified*, *distinguished*, *superseded*, and *withdrawn*;
7. first-party topical subject/detail links resolved by EOIR interim-decision ID;
8. SQLite FTS5 plus portable citation/applicability JSONL exports; and
9. a status report that shows incoming/outgoing treatment and evidence rather than declaring a binary result.

The official reporter table is stored in its own `reporter_affected_decisions` table, separate from both opinion-derived edges and the publisher-disclaimed chart. Negative reporter codes raise a “needs scope review” signal; they are not promoted to an unqualified invalid-law declaration.

The supporting parser also resolves 783 occurrences from the publisher-disclaimed BIA Precedent Chart to 608 unique official manifest cases with no unresolved occurrence in the captured snapshot. It preserves 780 unique case/topic links, 190 explicit treatment phrases in charted headnotes, and 16 incoming treatment annotations written into case headings. These are stored in separate secondary-evidence tables and never silently promoted to opinion-derived treatment.

## Remaining legal-research limitation

Deterministic parsing can identify explicit authority and treatment language at high precision. It cannot reliably infer the exact proposition affected by every coded reporter entry, reconcile all indirect treatment through circuit or Supreme Court decisions, or decide whether a later statute, regulation, or manual revision has displaced or fully absorbed a holding. Those are later semantic-review tasks. The planned GPU document pipeline preserves enough page-level provenance to support them without turning model output into source truth.

## Implementation and OCR benchmark status

The current local build contains 3,320 published decisions, including 3,317 full-text cases and three explicit metadata-only publisher gaps. It preserves 19,307 page records, 45,900 rule citations, 22,218 rule-applicability links, and 19,912 I&N reporter-citation occurrences. Of the reporter occurrences, 18,340 (92.11%) resolve to an official corpus case. The treatment graph contains 821 opinion/publisher-derived mentions or edges in addition to 2,653 official reporter affected-decision edges. None of these counts implies that every edge has proposition-level scope review.

The OCR queue contains 10,605 pages across 2,351 cases. Its 200-page stratified gold set covers 192 cases: 50 hardest double-transcription pages, 50 legal/treatment-dense pages, 40 footnote/layout/hyphenation pages, 30 historical scans, 20 born-digital controls, and 10 official topical-index pages. The checksummed gold bundle has now been processed on the RTX 5090 and retrieved under a ledger-bound transport contract. The immutable result set contains 200 canonical transport envelopes and 600 separate engine layers, all pinned to worker commit `57caee14541133c9591d903e1434bd29f4398366`; no OCR text has been selected, voted, adjudicated, or imported as canonical case text.

A first 16-page, three-engine RTX 5090 benchmark now has complete structural coverage: 16 immutable result envelopes and 48 separate layers. PP-OCRv6 and PaddleOCR-VL 1.6 each returned 16 known non-truncated layers; OvisOCR2 returned 12 known non-truncated layers and four known-truncated layers after deterministic post-page `1` repetition reached its token cap. There is no unknown truncation. Median observed runtimes were approximately 0.46 seconds, 0.90 seconds, and 33.3 seconds respectively. Every layer remains unselected.

Visual review found that the four Ovis loops begin after the visible page content, but also found legally material, engine-specific citation errors and multi-column reading-order failures. The deterministic citation-review contract flags six of the 16 pages for legal-unit disagreement and retains 13 engine-unique canonical readings; it preserves the conflicting `3`, `8`, and `9 U.S.C. 1251(a)(2)` outputs rather than voting among them. On at least one reporter token, two engines agreed with each other while the scan appeared to support the third. This rules out simple majority voting. Production routing must combine deterministic legal-unit validation, layout-specific checks, explicit disagreement review, and the larger gold set. The subsequent accepted worker moved the conservative Ovis repetition guard into native generation-time stopping; every guard-triggered output remains truncated and non-selectable.

The full 200-page run confirms that conclusion at useful scale. PP-OCRv6 produced 200 known non-truncated layers; PaddleOCR-VL 1.6 produced 198 known non-truncated layers plus two non-selectable, known artifact-region truncations; and OvisOCR2 produced 175 known non-truncated layers plus 25 non-selectable, exact repetition-guard truncations. The two Paddle cases came from one retained layout crop generating 4,096 middle-dot glyphs while all six legal-text crops stopped naturally and the merged page text reached a clean page ending. They remain marked partial rather than being silently relabeled complete. There are no unknown truncations or other engine errors. Median per-page runtimes were 581 ms for PP-OCRv6, 733 ms for PaddleOCR-VL, and 3,421.5 ms for OvisOCR2.

The deterministic legal-citation review classifies 61 of 200 pages as having canonical legal-unit disagreement, 84 as unanimous after typography-only canonicalization, 29 as unanimous in exact written form, and 26 as containing no recognized legal unit. It retains 47 engine-unique canonical readings. On the 20 high-quality publisher-native-text controls, which are automated proxies rather than human gold, mean normalized character similarity was 0.988 for PP-OCRv6, 0.960 for PaddleOCR-VL, and 0.991 for OvisOCR2; canonical legal-citation precision/recall were about 0.97/0.98 for all three. These controls do not justify choosing a universal winner, especially because the hard historical scans are deliberately outside the trustworthy-native-text proxy. The next quality gate is human double transcription/adjudication of the 50 hardest pages, followed by error-category routing. A defensible production policy can use the coordinate-rich PP layer as a baseline, preserve generative layers as independent evidence, automatically normalize typography only, and require review whenever digits, reporter pages, section suffixes, subsection tokens, citation multiplicity, or layout order disagree.

One run-control limitation was also preserved rather than papered over. The OCR worker completed successfully, but the run's Ovis runtime-capture helper missed its required simultaneous live observation and timed out without emitting `capture-v2.json`; consequently the original controller truthfully records `goldAcceptance=false`. A separate immutable supplement validates the unchanged 200 envelopes against the transfer map, engine lock, result schema, exact Ovis guard evidence, and exact Paddle artifact evidence. It does not backfill the missing live capture or convert the failed controller summary into a success. The exact worker/configuration already has an independently accepted capture-v2 proof from the preceding service acceptance, but future batch controllers should add a bounded post-sentinel capture deadline and a clearer distinction between OCR-result acceptance and auxiliary runtime-capture acceptance.
